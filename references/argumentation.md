# Argumentation and structure

The following are writing defaults and judgment criteria, not official Part B formatting requirements. Choose an organization that reflects the main contribution of the current study.

## A1 Title: scope and substance

- **Signals**: A vague title or a string of method names; undefined new terminology; claims of being the first, unified, or universal.
- **Assess**: Check whether each object, scope claim, and method in the title is supported by the model, theory, or experiments. Determine whether the main advance concerns the problem, the method, or their relationship, rather than choosing a title because it sounds sophisticated.
- **Revise**: Prefer established terminology in the field and remove modifiers without a concrete meaning. Narrow any scope unsupported by evidence. If alternatives are needed, offer a few titles with different emphases and explain the contribution each foregrounds. Do not require the sentence pattern “An algorithm for a problem.”
- **Recheck**: Can the manuscript meet the expectations created by the title alone? Does the title introduce contributions or abbreviations absent from the main text?

## A2 Sections and opening guidance: explain what the section accomplishes

- **Signals**: Headings merely list technical components; a section heading leads immediately into equations or subsections; section openings repeatedly restate the abstract.
- **Assess**: Check whether the section sequence develops from problem to model, method, and evidence, and whether readers can identify the current section's purpose and its relationship to the preceding text.
- **Revise**: For sections containing several substantive subsections, the default is a brief opening that explains the section's purpose and how its parts connect. A short subsection serving a single purpose may not need an additional introduction. This opening should not repeat the summary of results.
- **Recheck**: The opening contains information specific to the section, headings match their content, and mechanical use of “This section presents...” in every subsection does not create repetition.

Possible headings include Introduction; Literature review; Problem description and formulation; Solution method; Computational experiments; and Conclusions. These provide functional vocabulary, not a fixed number, order, or wording of sections. Separate the model from its properties, or the framework from the core method, when the study calls for it. Do not impose sections on managerial implications, industry cases, or a particular class of algorithms.

See the actual examples in [P1, the literature review opening on p. 5 and the algorithm section opening on p. 17](partb-evidence.md). First examine how they explain the section's purpose and internal relationships; then choose headings for the current manuscript. Do not copy the headings or require the same number of sections.

## A3 Introduction: derive the gap from decision difficulties

- **Signals**: The opening immediately introduces the manuscript's model; the background discusses only the industry's importance; the stated difficulty merely repeats the problem definition; detailed experimental procedures appear before the contributions.
- **Assess**: Label the argumentative role of each existing paragraph and check the connections among importance, concrete difficulties, decisions and trade-offs, the scope of existing approaches, the research gap, and the study's contributions. This is a logical relationship, not a fixed six-paragraph template.
- **Revise**: Extract concrete decisions and their consequences from the available material. Explain which objects receive the benefits and incur the costs, and how each is measured. Introduce the necessary concepts before new mechanisms, costs, or constraints. Summarize the literature by research direction and move detailed paper-by-paper comparisons to the review. Retain only the major experimental findings needed to establish the study's position.
- **Recheck**: A clear research rationale remains after promotional statements about the industry are removed. The gap is supported by the boundaries of prior work, and there are no unsourced facts or conclusions the manuscript has yet to establish. A section roadmap usually follows the contributions, without another summary of the entire paper.

Refer to the connections among operating mechanisms, decisions, difficulties, and the gap in [P1, pp. 4–5](partb-evidence.md). Its gap statements and contribution wording also require independent assessment: publication does not justify claiming the same gap or innovation for the current study.

## A4 Contributions: match each advance to its evidence

- **Signals**: Modeling, programming, testing, or plotting is itself listed as an innovation; one result is split into several contributions; a limited comparison becomes a claim of overall superiority; all findings are dismissed because one comparison is missing.
- **Assess**: For each claim, establish a working mapping among the relevant prior-work baseline, the new insight/capability/method, the location of supporting evidence, and the scope of applicability. Distinguish the value of the problem, theoretical results, algorithm design, and empirical findings; the study need not contribute in all four categories.
- **Revise**: Organize core results, direct corollaries, and algorithmic applications by their dependencies, removing double counting. Restrict comparative conclusions to the objects and conditions actually studied. Remove unsupported adjectives while retaining genuine advances. Do not split contributions merely to increase their number.
- **Recheck**: Contribution wording, theorem conditions, implemented methods, and experimental scope agree. Contributions are neither overstated nor narrowed without justification.

When choosing terminology, first compare the objects, conditions, and quantifiers. A general rule should identify the objects for which it holds; a restricted setting should identify the theoretical framework and the additional restrictions; a concrete example illustrates only a particular input or instance. Use “special case” only when an inclusion or restriction relationship has been established, not merely because a rule is simple. Likewise, covering every object in a subclass does not justify claiming coverage of a larger class.

## A5 Literature review: organize relationships among research findings

- **Signals**: Papers are listed only by author and year, without themes or comparative relationships; every paragraph ends mechanically with “However, this study...”; not considering the current study's setting is treated as a universal shortcoming; citations are added to meet a numerical target.
- **Assess**: First identify the research directions relevant to the current study, then compare problem features, assumptions, methods, or difficulties. Check whether every contrast reflects a real difference and whether that difference actually affects the decisions or solution process in this study.
- **Revise**: Open with a paragraph explaining how the review's scope relates to the research question. Center paragraphs on comparable lines of research, explaining established results and the conditions they depend on before drawing the gaps together. Replace “did not consider” criticisms with objective descriptions of scope and, where necessary, explain why the distinction matters for the current problem. Do not manufacture a gap when the field already has an answer.
- **Recheck**: The main research directions receive appropriate coverage. Citations appear near the specific claims they support. Readers can distinguish facts from the literature, the authors' synthesis, and material still requiring verification. The coverage needed determines the number of citations.

When a literature comparison table is useful, choose columns for dimensions that genuinely distinguish studies and can be verified objectively. Objectives and algorithms usually warrant concise text; use check marks only for clearly defined binary features. Unknown does not mean “no,” and absence from a title or abstract does not establish absence from the full paper. Avoid a series of advantage columns designed specifically to favor the current study.

See [P1, pp. 5–8](partb-evidence.md): it first separates the relevant research directions and then addresses their intersection. Use this organization only when the current manuscript has corresponding research strands. Within a topic, author-led sentences and local chronological ordering can be appropriate; neither is inherently an error.

## A6 Abstract and conclusions: consistency of claims throughout the manuscript (supplementary guidance)

- **Signals**: The abstract contains numbers or contributions absent from the main text; the conclusions repeat a list of sections; future work implies that a problem presented as solved remains unsolved.
- **Assess**: Extract the research question, key methods, principal findings, and boundaries from the completed main text, and check whether they support the abstract and conclusions.
- **Revise**: Present these elements concisely in the abstract; include specific numerical results only after verification. In the conclusions, summarize the new understanding, implications supported by evidence, and actual limitations, without introducing new mechanisms, proofs, or experimental conclusions. Select future work according to the study's needs rather than listing it mechanically.
- **Recheck**: The title, abstract, contributions, and conclusions use consistent scope and terminology. If an abstract word limit or journal format must be established, verify it separately in the official guidance.
